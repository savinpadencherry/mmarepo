The MMA Design Guidelines

A cohesive visual language for a premium, architect-facing furniture brand. Everything here — the mark, the palette, the type, the motion, the voice — is the single source of truth that carries from the website into every brand touchpoint. The design tokens live in assets/brand.css and are inherited by every page.

01 · The Mark

An arch, a keystone, a wordmark.

The mark is a minimal architectural arch with a central keystone axis and a gold keystone dot — a nod to MMA's architect-facing market and to the idea that every piece is built around a considered core. The wordmark pairs Cormorant Garamond with an italic gold Design.

Three locked variants: light (on paper), on-dark (on ink, keystone shifts to gold-light), and compact (3D tools, 18px wordmark). The arch inherits the surrounding ink colour; the keystone axis and dot stay gold.

Clear space

Keep a margin equal to the mark's height (30px) clear on every side. Never let another element enter the arch's inner curve. Minimum rendered size: 24px mark / 18px wordmark.

02 · Colour

A single gold, grounded in ink and paper.

One signature gold carries the brand across every surface. It is always paired with the near-black ink and a warm paper ground. Greens, blues and saturated hues are not part of the system — restraint is the point.

03 · Typography

A serif that feels, a sans that works.

Cormorant Garamond carries emotion — headlines, product names, quotes. Inter carries information — body, navigation, specs. The two never mix within a line; the italic gold em is the only display embellishment.

04 · Motion & System

Slow out, never bouncy.

Motion is restrained and confident — a single ease carries reveals, hovers and transitions. Reduced-motion is honoured everywhere: parallax, Ken Burns and floating cells switch off, reveals collapse to near-instant fades.

05 · Tone of Voice

Confident, considered, never loud.

MMA speaks to architects and design leaders who recognise craft without being told. The voice is precise, warm and quietly authoritative — the same register as a good architect explaining a detail.

01

Precise, not technical

Name the material, the dimension, the mechanism. Never the adjective pile-up. "Chamfered solid-timber slab" — not "luxuriously crafted premium surface".

02

Warm, not casual

Write like a trusted collaborator, not a salesperson. "Specify straight runs, corners and islands" invites the designer in.

03

Quiet, not modest

Let the work carry the claim. "The definitive executive chair" is enough — no exclamation marks, no superlatives for their own sake.

Do write

"A statement boardroom table with a chamfered solid-timber slab and architectural steel legs. Integrated power keeps the surface clear."

Don't write

"Our amazing premium boardroom table is crafted with the finest materials for the ultimate meeting experience!!!"
